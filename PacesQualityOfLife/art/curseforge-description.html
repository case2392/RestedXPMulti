Pace's Quality of Life

Small conveniences for World of Warcraft Forever's own windows. Each one can be switched off in Options > AddOns > Pace's Quality of Life, or with /pqol.

Quest log: track or untrack a whole zone

  • Every zone header in the Map & Quest Log (Redridge Mountains, Ashenvale, Blackfathom Deeps...) gets a tracking checkbox like the ones on the quests. Click it to track every quest in that zone, click again to untrack them all. A faint tick means some of the zone is tracked.
  • The Quests: 34/40 box above the list has one for all quests, and the list's gear menu has Track all quests and Untrack all quests.
  • The game tracks at most 25 quests at once, so "track all" fills up to 25, the zones on the open map first, and says how many didn't fit. A box that has filled what it can counts as full: its next click untracks.

Looking For Group: players by role

  • Three role buttons (tank, healer, damage) sit between Send Message and Group Invite. One click on the healer shows only healers. Click others to add or remove roles; clear the last one to see everyone again.
  • While a filter is on, the matching players show in a list of their own with its own Send Message and Group Invite buttons. Select a player, then click; a double-click whispers.
  • Blizzard's own list is never changed. Inside dungeons, raids and encounters the game hides this data from add-ons, so the filter steps aside there and Blizzard's list shows as usual.

Objective tracker follows your zone

  • When you enter a zone, the tracker switches to that zone's quests: everything under the zone's quest log header is tracked, the rest untracked. Go to Duskwood and you see Duskwood's quests.
  • It happens once per zone: anything you track or untrack by hand stays until you enter the next zone (a /reload doesn't count).
  • A dungeon's quests show inside the dungeon; the quest your arrow points to stays tracked; a zone with none of your quests (a city) leaves the tracker as it was; nothing changes during combat or a flight (it catches up after).

Chat Channels: who's in voice, before you join

  • Forever's Chat Channels window only shows who is in a community's voice channel once you're in it. With Pace's QoL, members in the voice channel get a microphone next to their name before you join.
  • The game doesn't tell add-ons who's in a voice channel you haven't joined, so Pace's QoL users tell each other: opening Chat Channels on a community asks the members an add-on can reach - your Battle.net friends playing Forever, your guild and your group, and in a WoW community the members of your faction - and the ones in voice who also run Pace's QoL answer, and say when they join or leave. Ask your friends to install it.
  • These are invisible add-on messages: nothing appears in chat, and nothing is sent in dungeons or encounters (a join or leave made there goes out once you're out). You can stop answering in the options.
  • A microphone missing? /pqol voice on both sides says what was sent, what came back and what the game refused (no names).
  • A channel you joined earlier this session and left with the headset shows everyone in it, add-on or not: the game still knows who's there.

Voice chat: each person's volume, kept

  • Right-click someone in voice (or their party frame, in party voice) and the slider sets how loud they are - but the game doesn't keep it. Pace's QoL remembers the volume you set for each person and sets it again whenever they're in voice with you: when they join, when you join, after a /reload and the next time you play.
  • One volume per person: a Battle.net friend in a Battle.net community with you, set from their party frame and from the community's roster, gets the one you set last.
  • Only what you set with the slider is kept, never the game's own resets. In dungeons and raids nothing is set (the game's chat lockdown); a change you make there is kept once you're out, not undone. From a member list opened in there (Chat Channels, Communities) the game doesn't say whose a change is: it is kept only for someone whose volume was kept before, and not at 100%, where everyone starts. /pqol voicevolume forget forgets them all.

Party frames: your party's pets

  • The standard party frames have a pet frame under each member, but WoW Forever's options have no switch for it. Tick "Party frames: show your party members' pets" and your party's pets show - after your next /reload (the options page has a Reload now button), so the party frames take it cleanly and keep working in combat.
  • It is the game's own setting, so it stays as you left it. Untick to hide them again. Each character's switch is its own. If the game refuses the switch, you're told what to type instead. For the raid-style party frames, use their own pet option.

Edit Mode: each computer its own layout

  • Edit Mode's layouts are your account's, and so is which one each character has on: play on your laptop after your PC and you get the PC's layout. Pace's QoL keeps a layout for each computer and switches to it as you log in.
  • Set it up once on each computer: pick its layout in Edit Mode, then press Use current in the options (or /pqol layout use). Every character logging in on that computer then gets its layout.
  • The switch reloads the UI once, so the game applies the layout as its own and your bars keep working in combat - only at a login, and only when the layout isn't the computer's already. Never in combat. A layout you put on during a session stays until you log out, a /reload included. "This computer" is its own WoW settings folder, so don't copy that folder between computers.

Auction house: Gold finder

  • A window that says what makes you gold right now, from your own recipes and the auction house's prices. Open it with /pqol gold or the Gold finder button above the auction house's top right corner. It never buys, bids, posts or cancels - it reads and adds up; you do the buying and selling.
  • Crafts: every recipe of yours that makes an item - what the materials cost (the cheapest of auctions, a vendor or your own craft), what it sells for (the best of auction, vendor or disenchanting), the profit, how many are listed and how many you can make. Recipes are read from your profession windows: open each one once on each character. A checkbox shows what each enchant's materials cost instead.
  • Bargains: auctions worth buying - listed for less than a vendor pays, gear under what it disenchants into, stackable items at 70% or less of their usual price, your recipes' materials cheaper than usual, and essences worth combining or splitting - with the price to buy up to. Only from prices less than an hour old.
  • Vendors: things a vendor sells that go for at least 20% more on the auction house after the cut. Vendors' prices are remembered as you visit them.
  • Your bags: for each item, what a vendor pays, what it sells for at auction and what it disenchants into - and the best of the three, added up under the list (sums that rest on an unproven auction price are left out, and counted beside the totals).
  • Prices are the add-on's own, three ways: Scan reads every auction once (the game allows it about once every 15 minutes; the button counts down); Price my items looks up just what your recipes and bags use, one item a second, and works during Scan's wait; and what you browse yourself is read as it goes by. A whole scan also settles what isn't listed at all, so nothing is left to look up straight after one. Your own auctions aren't counted (one exception: a stackable item another character of yours has up at a price other sellers share - that price stays); when a scan can't tell which are yours, the window says what to press to put that right.
  • A selling price is the lowest listed now, or the item's usual price if that is lower, so one hopeful listing doesn't count - and neither does one cheap lot (a quarter of what's listed at most, under what a vendor pays or at 70% or less of the usual price): that is a bargain to buy, not your price. A * marks a price that isn't proven (one or two listed, all at one price, or sold out before it had a usual price - then the price it was last seen at) or an item with none listed now. Prices over 48 hours old show their age and aren't used as today's.
  • Auctionator is optional: when it's installed, its recent prices fill in where Pace's QoL has none, and the Search button uses its search. Without it, Search puts the item's name in the auction house's search box and you click Search. TradeSkillMaster is not used.
  • The auction house's cut is counted as 5% (/pqol gold cut 10 changes it). Disenchanting values, essence conversions and the built-in vendor prices are vanilla's numbers: estimates (a profit resting on one has a "~", a built-in vendor price is called "not confirmed"), not checked on Forever.
  • Prices, recipes and vendors' prices are kept in the add-on's own saved settings on your computer. /pqol gold forget forgets this realm's prices.

Commands

  • /pqol - the options page
  • /pqol status - what's on, and any errors this session
  • /pqol questlog|lfgroles|zonetracker|voice|voicevolume|partypets|layout|gold on|off - switch a feature
  • /pqol voice - what voice sharing sent, heard and was refused (/pqol voice log: the last messages)
  • /pqol voicevolume forget - forget every volume kept
  • /pqol layout use - make the Edit Mode layout on now this computer's (/pqol layout forget: stop switching)
  • /pqol gold - open or close the gold finder (/pqol gold scan: read the whole auction house; /pqol gold price: look up the items your recipes and bags use, again to stop)
  • /pqol gold cut <0-30> - the auction house's cut in percent (/pqol gold forget: forget this realm's prices; /pqol gold help: the list)
